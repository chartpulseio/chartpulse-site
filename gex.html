How to read it. Cyan bars are strikes where dealers are long gamma (they sell rallies and buy dips, so price tends to stick); purple bars are short gamma (they chase moves, so price tends to slip through). Above the zero-gamma flip the tape is usually calmer; below it, moves extend. The dots show the options profile you pick (delta, gamma, vanna or charm exposure) split into calls (green) and puts (red). Max change GEX shows which strike moved most over the last 1 to 30 minutes, and the replay slider plays back today's positioning minute by minute.
Method. Exposure = gamma × open interest × contract size × spot² × 1%, calls positive and puts negative (the standard assumption that customers buy options and dealers take the other side). It is a model of positioning, not a certainty. Open interest updates once a day for stocks and indices; US quotes use Alpaca's indicative feed. Crypto: BTC and ETH come from Deribit, where most crypto options trade. In crypto, large holders often sell calls themselves, so the dealer side can differ from the stock-market assumption; treat crypto walls as zones of heavy positioning rather than certain support or resistance.
